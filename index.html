IAT 410 Blog Updates

By Mark Yang

Project Manager for Team Zootopia

Hi, I'm a project manager and one of the programmers for Team Zootopia. I help keep the team organized and on track, while also working on the technical side of our game — spefically the gameplay systems and mechanics.

I've always loved games, as a player and as a designer. I enjoy thinking about how games feel to play and how even small mechanics can shape the overall experience. Ourside of game development, I'm into music — both listening and playing whenever I have the chance.

This blog is where I'll be documenting my personal progress, relfections, and lessons learned throughout this project.

Week 1–2: Project Setup

Over the past two weeks, I’ve been getting more comfortable with the project and the team workflow. Early on, things felt a bit uncertain — there was a lot on the table. Our team already had a concept and designs in mind going into the project, but more in terms of mechanics and game direction, and it took some time to understand how everything would actually come together. Through group discussions and meetings, I’m starting to see the bigger picture more clearly and how each part of the project connects.

Personally, I’ve just been focusing on listening, asking questions, and figuring out where my strengths fit best within the team. Taking on the role of PM has meant thinking a lot about organization and keeping things on track, while also making sure everyone is on the same page. I was also in charge of setting up and maintaining the team blog, which has been a good way to document our progress and keep things transparent for the group.

I’m also one of the programmers on the team, because of that, I — along with a couple of the other programmers — have been keeping the team on track in terms of scoping. The beginning of our ideation was extremely ambitious, so we ended up having to cut down on a lot of mechanics and decide which ones to keep in order of importance — ultimately deciding to work on the main game concepts like the platforming and visual novel aspects to focus on clean movement, and one strong mechanic feature, the MOOD combat system. For the next iteration, I want to start digging into tools like Unity and learning how to implement some of the core mechanics and overall feel of the game. Since our game is leaning toward a mix of side-scrolling, visual novel, and platformer elements, I’ll be focusing mainly on the platforming side for now. My goal is to get movement feeling clean and responsive so we have a solid foundation to build on.

Moving forward, I want to be more proactive both as a PM and as a programmer — helping keep deliverables on track while also contributing more technically. OVerall, the team and their ideas have been super fun and I’m excited to start turning our ideas into something playable and to learn from whatever challenges come up along the way, smile.

Weeks 3–6: From blueprint to building

Weeks 3–6 were where things really started to amalgamate. Instead of just talking about ideas, we finally moved into implementing them. This included building our first playable and working on core mechanics like player movement, environment interactions, scaling, combat, UI, and interaction systems.

A big thing for me personally is that this was my first time properly using Git, Unity, and writing gameplay scripts. There were a lot of long, sometimes pretty grueling hours trying to figure things out (and many a cup of coffee along the way), but somehow we managed to scrape by. A lot of this phase was trial and error — fixing one bug just to uncover three more — but each problem taught me something new.

Most of my time went into building the player controller and gameplay systems; debugging movement issues, wall sliding, physics behaving strangely, and how different systems interacted with each other. There were definitely moments where something small would break everything, but slowly things started to come together. Seeing the character move around in an actual level instead of an empty scene was honestly super rewarding.

On top of that, I made a few placeholder sprites to test with while building mechanics. They weren’t meant to be permanent, but I honestly got kind of attached to them — I’ll miss these little guys.

My teammates also made solid progress during this time by adding more enemy types, implementing text and interaction for the side scroller, and creating UI elements and artwork. These additions helped the game feel more complete and gave us a better sense of how everything fits together.

This was also our first real experience working collaboratively using GitHub and GitHub Desktop, and it was… messy at first, and honestly we still aren't perfect. Since most of us were beginners, we ran into merge conflicts, workflow confusion, and even had a situation where bugs were pushed into main and we had to backtrack. Someone attempted to rebase instead of pulling main and fixing things locally, which made things more complicated than they needed to be. On top of that, some team members weren’t always delivering their parts on time, which caused delays and made merging stressful right before the deadline.

Even though it wasn’t smooth, it ended up being a really important learning experience. I definitely have a better understanding now of how version control works in a team setting, and how important communication and consistent workflows are. It wasn’t our cleanest milestone, but it taught all of us a lot.

Overall, these weeks pushed me to grow both technically and as a project manager. I learned a lot about debugging, iteration, and keeping things moving forward even when stuff breaks. We now have a playable build, and moving into the next phase, I’m excited to focus more on polish, feel, and refining mechanics. It’s cool seeing our project slowly turn into something tangible.
